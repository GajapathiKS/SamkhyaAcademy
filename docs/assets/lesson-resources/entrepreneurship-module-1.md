# Entrepreneurship & Venture Builder

## Module 1: Idea and Opportunity

Use a fictional crop-advisory business to learn venture design. You may also use your own case; taking the course does not submit that idea to the academy.

### 1.1 Opportunity framing

Frame an opportunity as a change in a specific customer situation, not a list of product features. Identify the triggering event, current workaround and consequence. An interesting technology is not evidence of demand.

### 1.2 Problem clarity

A problem statement names who experiences the problem, what happens and why it matters. Separate observations from assumptions. Avoid combining several unrelated audiences into one statement because their urgency and purchasing behavior may differ.

### 1.3 Target customer

Choose a narrow initial segment that can be reached and studied. Distinguish the user, buyer and decision-maker. A farmer using advice and an organization paying for it may need different evidence and different value propositions.

### 1.4 Mentor selection review

In the course, review a problem brief against clarity, evidence and uncertainty. This is an assignment review, not venture admission. The separate Submit an idea journey requests evaluation for mentor-supported venture progression; it has its own decision and status.

## Worked lab

Case hypothesis:
Segment: growers managing small irrigated vegetable plots
Situation: deciding when to irrigate after irregular rainfall
Current workaround: personal observation and calls to peers
Unknown: frequency, cost of mistakes and willingness to change
Do not replace these unknowns with invented market evidence.

A precise hypothesis tells you whom to interview and what behavior to investigate. It is still a hypothesis until evidence is collected.
