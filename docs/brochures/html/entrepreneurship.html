Detailed module curriculum · 5-7

Apply, integrate and demonstrate.

The module names, lesson sequence and evidence below match the course learning workspace.

5Pilot Launch

Plan a limited pilot with a clear audience, support boundary and rollback decision.

  1. 5.1Pilot designA pilot has a limited scope, duration, participant set and purpose.
  2. 5.2Go-to-market experimentA go-to-market experiment tests one channel and one message with a defined audience.
  3. 5.3Launch checklistA launch checklist covers access, support, privacy, incident handling, ownership and data recovery.
  4. 5.4Launch gateA launch gate compares evidence with pre-agreed readiness criteria.

You will produce: Pilot plan, acquisition experiment and launch-readiness review.

6Traction and Learning

Use evidence to decide what to improve, continue or stop.

  1. 6.1MetricsChoose a metric that represents the behavior the venture needs, not merely activity.
  2. 6.2Feedback loopsA feedback loop connects observation to a prioritized change and a follow-up measurement.
  3. 6.3Experiment resultsReport experiment results against the original question and acknowledge sample limitations.
  4. 6.4Traction reviewA traction review combines quantitative behavior, customer evidence and operating costs.

You will produce: Metric dictionary, feedback log and traction review.

7Scale and Production

Prepare a responsible operating plan and present the evidence behind the venture.

  1. 7.1Operating modelAn operating model assigns decisions, delivery work, support and escalation.
  2. 7.2Security and compliance readinessIdentify sensitive data, access boundaries and obligations that require qualified advice.
  3. 7.3Scale planA scale plan connects demand assumptions with capacity, cost and quality.
  4. 7.4Final showcaseThe final showcase presents the problem, evidence, model, prototype, pilot results and remaining risks.

You will produce: Operating plan, scale scenario and final course showcase.

Inside module 1: a worked example

Case hypothesis:
Segment: growers managing small irrigated vegetable plots
Situation: deciding when to irrigate after irregular rainfall
Current workaround: personal observation and calls to peers
Unknown: frequency, cost of mistakes and willingness to change
Do not replace these unknowns with invented market evidence.

A precise hypothesis tells you whom to interview and what behavior to investigate. It is still a hypothesis until evidence is collected.

How your work is reviewed

Show the artifact, explain your decisions, test the stated assumptions and identify limitations. Module assignments build toward the course project rather than replacing it with a single example.
